## What does Artificial Intelligence mean?

Artificial Intelligence is the study and practice of making computer systems perform tasks that need useful judgement. This may involve recognising patterns, searching possibilities, understanding language, planning actions, or learning from examples. A machine need not think exactly like a person: the useful question is whether it can act effectively for a stated purpose.

## Why does AI matter?

AI influences health, finance, agriculture, transport, education, media, and public services. Nigerian examples could include an app helping farmers identify crop disease from a photo, a payment service flagging unusual transactions, or a university help desk routing questions to the right office. These systems can help, but their value depends on reliable data, safe design, and human oversight.

## Major approaches to defining AI

- **Thinking humanly:** modelling aspects of human cognition.
- **Acting humanly:** behaving in ways people judge intelligent.
- **Thinking rationally:** following sound rules of reasoning.
- **Acting rationally:** selecting actions that best serve a goal with available information.

The right approach depends on the task, the data, and the consequences of error.

## AI versus Machine Learning

Machine learning is part of AI that learns patterns from data. A rule that alerts when a water level crosses a threshold can be useful automation without learning. A model trained to classify plant-leaf images uses machine learning. AI also includes search, planning, logic, and knowledge representation.

## Intelligent agents and PEAS

An agent observes an environment through **sensors** and affects it through **actuators**. A **performance measure** defines success.

| PEAS element | Campus navigation assistant example |
| --- | --- |
| Performance measure | Accurate routes, accessible paths, low user confusion |
| Environment | Roads, buildings, traffic, weather, user requests |
| Actuators | Map display, route notifications, spoken directions |
| Sensors | GPS location, map updates, user input, traffic data |

## Ethics from the beginning

AI can exclude people when data is incomplete, repeat bias, expose private information, or inspire unwarranted trust. Ask: Who is affected? What data is collected? How are errors corrected? Who is accountable? Judge systems by fairness, privacy, safety, and local fit as well as accuracy.
